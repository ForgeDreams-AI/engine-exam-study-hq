# Fire Engineer Study Pack — Scenario Scripts

Four interactive decision-game scenarios. Read each step, pick a choice, then check the answer.
**☠ FAIL** = the scenario ends immediately — these mirror real test failures.

# Scenario A: Phase 2 Key Pumper

**Briefing:** You are the candidate. First engineer of drivers training is the forward pumper. Second engineer will handjack supply hose to you. You make ALL connections. Run the full evolution: put the truck in pump, work the humat, manage the valve sequence, take the 2nd water supply, and shut down clean. Time starts when your foot hits the ground and stops when the truck is shut down.

## Step A1 — Getting started
**Situation:** You're in the driver's seat. What do you do?
- A) Put the truck in pump, exit with safety vest and radio
- B) Exit with vest and radio, then put the truck in pump from outside
- C) Deploy hose first, then put the truck in pump
- D) Put the truck in pump and exit with just the radio
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — properly put it in pump first, exit with vest AND radio.
**If wrong:** Wrong. The exercise begins by properly putting the truck in pump, then exiting with a safety vest and radio.

## Step A2 — First priority
**Situation:** Your foot hits the ground. Time is running. What's first?
- A) Place wheel chocks
- B) Walk to the rear and pull the supply line
- C) Pump the humat — deploy the first hose length to the front of the humat
- D) Radio the practor for orders
**Correct: C**
**If right:** Correct — first priority is properly pumping the humat.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Chocks and the practor come later. First priority is the humat.

## Step A3 — Humat couplings
**Situation:** First hose length is on the front of the humat. Next?
- A) Grab the double male, attach to the hose, deploy second length and connect it via the double male
- B) Connect the second length directly to the first with a double female
- C) Run the second length straight to your intake
- D) Open the discharge before finishing couplings
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — double male on the hose, second length connected through it.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Hose-to-hose uses the double male from the compartment.

## Step A4 — Finishing the humat side
**Situation:** Both hose lengths are connected. What's the last humat connection?
- A) Grab the second double male, connect it to your intake, then to your second hose
- B) Connect your intake directly to the second hose
- C) Use a double female between intake and hose
- D) Open the butterfly valve now
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — second double male: intake first, then to the second hose.
**If wrong:** Wrong. The intake-to-hose connection takes the second double male.

## Step A5 — Rear supply line
**Situation:** You walk to the rear and pull a length of supply line. Which end goes where?
- A) Male end to truck discharge (passenger N.1), female end to side of humat
- B) Female end to truck discharge (passenger N.1), male end to side of humat
- C) Female end to the intake, male end to the discharge
- D) Either end — they're interchangeable here
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — female to the discharge (passenger N.1), male to the side of the humat.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Female end to the truck discharge (passenger N.1); male end to the side of the humat.

## Step A6 — Valve sequence (critical)
**Situation:** All connections are made. In what order do you open them?
- A) Intake (keystone) → butterfly valve → discharge
- B) Discharge connected to the humat FIRST → flip the butterfly valve → open the intake (keystone)
- C) Butterfly valve → intake → discharge
- D) Open everything at once to save time
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — discharge to humat first, butterfly, then intake (keystone). Order is critical.
**If wrong:** Wrong. The sequence is discharge-to-humat FIRST, butterfly valve, THEN intake (keystone).

## Step A7 — Pump pressure
**Situation:** Water is flowing. The forward pumper hasn't called for more yet. What pressure?
- A) Maximum 150 psi right away
- B) Equal pressure to your intake pressure
- C) 100 psi
- D) Idle — no pressure needed yet
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — match intake pressure until told otherwise. Max 150 psi only after the call.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Pump at equal pressure to intake until the forward pumper calls for more (max 150 psi).

## Step A8 — Second water supply couplings
**Situation:** The drivers training engineer handjacks a supply line to your truck — a reverse operation. What couplings do you need?
- A) Double male only
- B) Double female only
- C) Double male AND double female
- D) A reducer
**Correct: C**
**If right:** Correct — every reverse operation needs double male AND double female.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Every reverse operation requires both a double male and a double female.

## Step A9 — Key pumper discharge connection
**Situation:** In this reverse operation, which coupling connects to the discharge from the key pumper?
- A) The double male
- B) The double female
- C) The butterfly valve
- D) The intake coupling
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — from the key pumper, the double female connects to the discharge.
**If wrong:** Wrong. From the key pumper, the double female goes to the discharge.

## Step A10 — Ready for water (critical)
**Situation:** Connections for the 2nd supply are made. The forward pumper needs water NOW. You:
- A) Open the discharge immediately — speed matters
- B) Communicate face-to-face or over the radio that you are ready for water, then open the discharge
- C) Wait for the practor to tell you to open it
- D) Increase to 150 psi first, then open
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — readiness communicated first, then open the discharge.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — Sending water before communicating readiness violates Critical Factor #3 (receiving trucks ready before sending water). Scenario over.

## Step A11 — Wheel chocks
**Situation:** Discharge is open, you're standing by for the practor's orders. Now what?
- A) Place wheel chocks — this is the appropriate time
- B) Shut down the key system
- C) Close all gates
- D) Take the truck out of pump
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — now is the appropriate time for chocks.
**If wrong:** Wrong. With the discharge open and orders pending, place wheel chocks.

## Step A12 — Shutdown begins (critical)
**Situation:** The practor calls for shutdown. Crews are still reported inside the building. You:
- A) Shut down the lines immediately — the practor gave the order
- B) Bring the pump to 100 psi and idle first, then check on crews
- C) Refuse — verify ALL crews are accounted for and out of the building before shutting down any lines
- D) Open tank fill to start cooling while crews exit
**Correct: C**
**If right:** Correct — crews out, accounted for, and safe BEFORE any line is shut down.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — Shutting down with crews inside violates Critical Factor #2. Scenario over.

## Step A13 — Shutdown pressure
**Situation:** All crews are out and accounted for. Other engineers have been told shutdown is beginning. Next?
- A) Bring the pump to 100 psi or less, then idle
- B) Idle immediately from 150 psi
- C) Close the intake first
- D) Shut down the truck
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — 100 psi or less before idle.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Bring it down to 100 psi or less BEFORE putting it in idle.

## Step A14 — Tank fill
**Situation:** All gates are closed. Next two actions in order?
- A) Open tank fill (fill tank + cool pump), then feel the water — close it only when cool
- B) Close tank fill immediately to save water
- C) Shut down the key system, then open tank fill
- D) Put away chocks, then open tank fill
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — tank fill cools the pump and fills the tank; water must feel cool before closing.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Open tank fill, feel the water on the ground, close it only when cool.

## Step A15 — Final sequence
**Situation:** Tank fill is closed, water was cool. Finish the evolution in order:
- A) Shut down key system → put away wheel chocks → take vehicle out of pump properly → shut down truck
- B) Shut down truck → put away chocks → key system
- C) Take out of pump → shut down truck → chocks stay
- D) Key system → shut down truck → chocks
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — key system, chocks away, out of pump properly, shut down truck. Time stops.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Key system → chocks → out of pump properly → shut down truck.

## Step A16 — 2-stage pump gear (critical)
**Situation:** Throughout this entire evolution on the 2-stage pump, what gear?
- A) Drive
- B) Park
- C) NEUTRAL — neutral going in / neutral going out
- D) Whatever it was left in
**Correct: C**
**If right:** Correct — Critical Factor #5.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — Wrong gear on a 2-stage pump. Neutral going in, neutral going out. Scenario over.

**Scenario A complete.** If you survived all 16 steps, you ran a clean Phase 2.

# Scenario B: Practical Test Run

**Briefing:** You're running the Engineer Practical Test Checklist start to finish. Verbalize the critical lines out loud as you go — the evaluator is listening. One wrong discharge, one kicked clamp, or one missed verbalization can end your day.

## Step B1 — Cab prep order
**Situation:** You're in the cab. What's the exact sequence?
- A) Start truck → engage PTO → shift to Drive → vest on → grab radio
- B) Seatbelt + vest on → start truck, verify Neutral → engage Dual Stage PTO → shift to Drive, verify two green lights → remove seatbelt, grab radio, look both ways
- C) Vest on → shift to Drive → verify green lights → engage PTO
- D) Start truck in Drive, then engage PTO
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — all five steps, in order. PTO engages in Neutral before Drive.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Seatbelt/vest → start, Neutral → PTO → Drive (two green lights) → seatbelt off, radio, look both ways.

## Step B2 — Front tire verbalization
**Situation:** You stop at the front tire. What do you say?
- A) "Intake above 20 PSI"
- B) "Wheel chocks will be set later"
- C) "Tank-to-Pump is open"
- D) Nothing — just keep moving
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — verbalize it now; chocks get set at the water-supply step.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Stop at the front tire and verbalize: "Wheel chocks will be set later."

## Step B3 — Crosslay and Tank-to-Pump
**Situation:** Hose bed is clear. Before opening a discharge at idle, you must:
- A) Identify the correct crosslay and verify Tank-to-Pump is OPEN
- B) Open the Keystone
- C) Set the wheel chocks
- D) Pump the handline immediately
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — identify the crosslay (say it out loud), verify Tank-to-Pump OPEN.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Identify the correct crosslay and verify Tank-to-Pump is OPEN before any discharge opens.

## Step B4 — Supply deployment
**Situation:** Time to establish water supply. You deploy:
- A) Two sections both to the Keystone
- B) One supply section to the top of the truck and one to the Keystone
- C) One section to the crosslay
- D) Everything to the top of the truck
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — one up top, one to the Keystone.
**If wrong:** Wrong. One supply section to the top of the truck, one to the Keystone.

## Step B5 — Hose clamp (critical)
**Situation:** The hose clamp needs opening. You:
- A) Kick it open — fastest method
- B) Open it facing the hazard, by hand — never kick it
- C) Leave it; the Keystone pressure will force it
- D) Open it facing away from the hazard
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — facing the hazard, by hand. Say it: "Do NOT kick the hose clamp open."
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — Kicking the clamp open is a critical error. Scenario over.

## Step B6 — Keystone
**Situation:** You open the Keystone. What do you verify, and what are your next three actions?
- A) Verify RPM drops; then crack Tank Fill and mark gauges, set wheel chocks, listen for radio traffic
- B) Verify RPM rises; then set chocks and shut down
- C) Verify pressure spikes; then open all discharges
- D) Nothing to verify — move to ladder ops
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — RPM drops on Keystone open; then Tank Fill + gauge marks, chocks, radio.
**If wrong:** Wrong. RPM must drop. Then: crack Tank Fill and mark gauges, set wheel chocks, listen for radio traffic.

## Step B7 — Ladder discharge (critical)
**Situation:** Ladder supply connections are made. Multiple discharges are in front of you. You:
- A) Open the closest one — they're all the same
- B) Verify you have identified the CORRECT discharge BEFORE opening anything
- C) Open one and check the gauge after
- D) Ask the ladder crew to pick
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — identify first, open second. Say the verbalization.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — Opening the wrong discharge is an AUTOMATIC FAIL. Scenario over.

## Step B8 — Ladder ready
**Situation:** Correct discharge identified. Before opening it, confirm:
- A) Ladder drains are open
- B) Ladder drains are closed and the ladder is ready for water
- C) The handline is fully shut down
- D) The Keystone is closed
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — "Ladder drains are closed and the ladder is ready for water."
**If wrong:** Wrong. Drains closed, ladder ready for water — verbalize it before opening.

## Step B9 — Pumping the ladder
**Situation:** Ladder is flowing. What do you do with the handline?
- A) Shut the handline down completely
- B) Pump the ladder at the briefed PSI while gating down the handline, referencing your gauge mark
- C) Leave the handline wide open
- D) Close the Keystone
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — gate the handline down, hold the briefed ladder PSI, reference the gauge mark.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Gate down the handline while pumping the ladder at the specified PSI; reference your gauge mark.

## Step B10 — Intake safety (critical)
**Situation:** You glance at the intake gauge: it's falling — 18 PSI and dropping. You:
- A) Keep pumping; it'll recover
- B) Immediately act to bring intake back above 20 PSI — never let it stay below
- C) Open another discharge to balance it
- D) Shut down the truck
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — intake must stay above 20 PSI. Fix it now.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — Intake below 20 PSI is a safety failure. Scenario over.

## Step B11 — Shutdown authorization (critical)
**Situation:** Time to shut down. Dispatch asks if you're ready. You:
- A) Begin shutdown — they'll get the PAR to you later
- B) Confirm PAR is complete and ALL crews are out of the hot zone before touching anything
- C) Reduce pressure first, confirm PAR after
- D) Close discharges, then check PAR
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — PAR complete + crews out of the hot zone BEFORE shutdown begins.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — Shutting down before PAR and crews-out is a critical failure. Scenario over.

## Step B12 — Shutdown pressure and Tank Fill
**Situation:** PAR confirmed, hot zone clear. Next?
- A) Reduce below 100 PSI and idle, close all discharge lines, open Tank Fill to fill the tank and cool the pump
- B) Shut the truck off immediately
- C) Open Tank Fill before reducing pressure
- D) Close the Keystone first
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — below 100 PSI, idle, discharges closed, Tank Fill open (two reasons: fill + cool).
**If wrong:** Wrong. Below 100 PSI → idle → close discharges → Tank Fill open.

## Step B13 — Cooling check
**Situation:** Tank Fill is open and circulating. You feel the discharge water — it's still hot. You:
- A) Close the Tank Fill anyway — time matters
- B) Keep circulating a few more minutes until the water feels cool
- C) Shut down the pump
- D) Open more discharges
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — if still hot, continue circulating. Feel the water; cool to the touch before closing.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Hot water means keep cooling — never close Tank Fill on a hot pump.

## Step B14 — Walk-around and Keystone
**Situation:** Water is cool. Next two steps in order?
- A) 360° walk-around, then close the Keystone
- B) Close the Keystone, then walk around
- C) Store chocks, then walk around
- D) Shut down truck, then walk around
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — 360° walk-around first, then close the Keystone.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Walk-around first, then close the Keystone.

## Step B15 — PTO shutdown (critical)
**Situation:** Panel verified, all valves closed, chocks stored. Disengage the pump:
- A) Neutral → wait 5 sec → PTO first position → wait 5 sec → PTO final position
- B) PTO off → Neutral → shut down
- C) Shift to Park, then PTO off
- D) PTO final position → Neutral
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — exact sequence with both 5-second waits. Then double-check gauges and shut down the truck.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — Wrong PTO sequence. Neutral, 5 sec, PTO first, 5 sec, PTO final. Scenario over.

**Scenario B complete.** Verbalize every critical line on test day — the evaluator can't credit what they don't hear.

# Scenario C: Pre-Trip Air Brake Test

**Briefing:** Walk the full 12-step air brake test in order. Every PSI value and every time limit counts. Mix up 2 vs 3, or cut-in vs cut-out, and you'll chase the wrong fault on a real rig.

## Step C1 — Steps 1–2
**Situation:** You approach the rig to begin the air brake test. First two actions?
- A) Start the engine, then set chocks
- B) Set wheel chocks properly; engine off, power and ignition on
- C) Pump the brake to 60 PSI, then set chocks
- D) Test the service brake first
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — chocks on, engine off with power and ignition on.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Step 1: chocks. Step 2: engine off, power and ignition on.

## Step C2 — Parking brake leakage
**Situation:** Parking brake pushed in (released). You watch the gauge for 1 minute. Maximum allowable drop?
- A) 1 PSI
- B) 2 PSI
- C) 3 PSI
- D) 5 PSI
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — 2 PSI max with the parking brake released.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Parking brake released = 2 PSI max in 1 minute. (3 PSI is the foot-brake test.)

## Step C3 — Service brake leakage
**Situation:** Foot brake applied. You watch for 1 minute. Maximum allowable initial drop?
- A) 2 PSI
- B) 3 PSI
- C) 4 PSI
- D) 6 PSI
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — 3 PSI max with the foot brake applied.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Foot brake applied = 3 PSI max in 1 minute. (2 PSI was the parking-brake test.)

## Step C4 — Low pressure warning
**Situation:** You pump the brake down. At what PSI must the low pressure warning signal activate?
- A) 40 PSI
- B) 60 PSI
- C) 80 PSI
- D) 100 PSI
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — warning at 60 PSI.
**If wrong:** Wrong. The low pressure warning activates at 60 PSI.

## Step C5 — Spring brakes
**Situation:** Keep bleeding air. Spring brakes should activate between:
- A) 10–20 PSI
- B) 20–40 PSI
- C) 40–60 PSI
- D) 60–80 PSI
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — 20–40 PSI.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Spring brakes: 20–40 PSI.

## Step C6 — Build-up rate
**Situation:** You start the truck. At what engine speed do you check the build-up rate?
- A) Idle
- B) High idle or 1500 RPM
- C) 2000 RPM
- D) Redline
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — high idle or 1500 RPM.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Build-up rate is checked at high idle or 1500 RPM.

## Step C7 — Build-up numbers
**Situation:** At high idle, the gauge climbs. What must it do?
- A) 85 to 100 PSI within 45 seconds, then build to 120–125 for cut-out
- B) 60 to 100 PSI within 60 seconds, then stop at 100
- C) 100 to 125 PSI within 45 seconds
- D) 0 to 120 PSI within 45 seconds
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — 85–100 PSI within 45 seconds, then let it build to 120–125 for cut-out.
**If wrong:** Wrong. 85 to 100 PSI within 45 seconds; then build to 120–125 PSI for cut-out.

## Step C8 — Cut-in check
**Situation:** To verify compressor cut-in, you:
- A) Pump the brake down to 100 PSI and watch for the air build-up
- B) Pump the brake down to 60 PSI and listen
- C) Rev to 1500 RPM and watch for 125 PSI
- D) Shut the engine off at 120 PSI
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — down to 100 PSI, watch it build. That's cut-in.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Cut-in: pump down to 100 PSI, watch for build-up. (120–125 is cut-OUT.)

## Step C9 — Cut-out check
**Situation:** Compressor cut-out must occur between:
- A) 100–110 PSI
- B) 110–120 PSI
- C) 120–125 PSI
- D) 125–135 PSI
**Correct: C**
**If right:** Correct — cut-out at 120–125 PSI.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Cut-out: 120–125 PSI. (100 PSI is cut-IN.)

## Step C10 — Finish
**Situation:** Air system checks are complete. Final three steps in order?
- A) Test parking brake → test service brake → remove chocks
- B) Remove wheel chocks → test parking brake → test service brake
- C) Test service brake → remove chocks → test parking brake
- D) Remove chocks → test service brake → test parking brake
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — chocks off (10), parking brake test (11), service brake test (12).
**If wrong:** Wrong. 10: remove chocks. 11: test parking brake. 12: test service brake.

**Scenario C complete.** Drill the pairs until they're automatic: 2 vs 3 PSI, 100 cut-in vs 120–125 cut-out.

# Scenario D: Code 3 Response

**Briefing:** You're driving the engine Code 3. Five rules govern everything you do. Each step puts you in a real street situation — pick the legal, by-the-book action.

## Step D1 — Open road
**Situation:** Posted limit 45 mph, clear four-lane road, you're running Code 3. Your speed?
- A) 45 mph — never exceed the limit
- B) Up to 55 mph — max 10 over posted
- C) 65 mph — emergency traffic will move
- D) 70 mph — lights and sirens clear the way
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — Rule 1: maximum 10 mph over posted. 45 + 10 = 55.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Rule 1 caps you at 10 mph over the posted limit — 55 mph here.

## Step D2 — Center lane
**Situation:** Traffic is stopped ahead. You move into the center turn lane to pass. What's your max speed, and what comes first?
- A) 10 over posted, no slowdown needed
- B) Brief deceleration period, then max 20 mph
- C) 35 mph steady
- D) Stop completely before entering
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — Rule 2: brief deceleration, then max 20 mph in center/oncoming lanes.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Decelerate briefly, then max 20 mph in the center lane.

## Step D3 — Red light, oncoming lane
**Situation:** You're in the oncoming lane approaching a red light. Intersection looks clear. You:
- A) Proceed through at 20 mph — it's clear
- B) Slow to 10 mph and roll through
- C) Make a complete stop, then proceed when safe
- D) Honk and continue
**Correct: C**
**If right:** Correct — Rule 3: complete stop at ALL lights regardless of color in center/oncoming lanes.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — Running it violates Rule 3. In the oncoming lane you stop at every light, red or green. Scenario over.

## Step D4 — Stop sign, oncoming lane
**Situation:** Still in the oncoming lane — now a stop sign, no cross traffic visible. You:
- A) Roll through at 20 mph
- B) Complete stop, then proceed
- C) 10 mph rolling stop is fine if clear
- D) Speed up to clear the lane faster
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — complete stop at all stop signs in center/oncoming lanes.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — Rule 3 again: complete stop, every time, regardless. Scenario over.

## Step D5 — With traffic, green light
**Situation:** You're back with traffic, approaching a green light. You:
- A) Stop anyway — Code 3 means stop at everything
- B) Proceed through — Rule 4 requires stops only at red lights and stop signs when with traffic
- C) Slow to 20 mph through the green
- D) Move to the oncoming lane to be safe
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — with traffic, Rule 4 covers red lights and stop signs only.
**If wrong:** Wrong. With traffic you stop at red lights and stop signs — a green light is fine to proceed through.

## Step D6 — School zone
**Situation:** Active school zone ahead, 25 mph posted, kids visible on the sidewalk. You:
- A) Maintain 35 mph — you're still 10 over
- B) Lights and sirens may stay on, but do NOT exceed 25 mph; consider dropping to Code 2
- C) Kill the lights and sirens entirely
- D) 20 mph max like the center lane rule
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — Rule 5: lights/sirens OK, never exceed the posted limit in an active school zone; consider Code 2.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — 10-over does NOT apply in an active school zone. Never exceed the posted limit; consider Code 2. Scenario over.

**Scenario D complete.** The pattern: center/oncoming = stop at everything, max 20 after decelerating; with traffic = reds and stop signs; school zones = posted limit is absolute.

# Scenario E: Loss of Supply Line

**Briefing:** You are pumping on a hydrant supply when the supply line fails. Interior crews are operating inside the structure on your water. The packet list is in a strict order — and the first four items come BEFORE you notify Command, because preserving water for the interior crews is the most urgent need. Get the order wrong and crews run dry.

## Step E1 — First action
**Situation:** The supply line just failed. Water to the interior crews is at risk. What is your FIRST action?
- A) Notify Command via emergency traffic
- B) Close the intake valve
- C) Open the "tank-to-pump" valve
- D) Increase the throttle
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — close the intake first, so tank water can't flow backwards from the pump.
**If wrong:** Wrong. The list is ordered and the first four come before notifying Command. First: close the intake valve.

## Step E2 — Second action
**Situation:** Intake is closed. Next?
- A) Shut down the exterior lines
- B) Open the "tank-to-pump" valve
- C) Notify Command via emergency traffic
- D) Replace the damaged hose section
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — open "tank-to-pump" so tank water flows to the pump.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Second: open the "tank-to-pump" valve to get tank water to the pump.

## Step E3 — Third action
**Situation:** Tank water is flowing to the pump. Next?
- A) Increase the throttle to maximum
- B) Notify Command via emergency traffic
- C) Shut down any exterior or exposure lines not being operated inside the structure
- D) Shut down the hydrant
**Correct: C**
**If right:** Correct — shut down exterior/exposure lines to preserve water for the interior crew(s).
**If wrong:** Wrong. Third: shut down exterior and exposure lines not in use inside, preserving water for the interior crews.

## Step E4 — Fourth action
**Situation:** Exterior lines are shut down. Next?
- A) The PCG increases the throttle to regain proper engine pressure for the lines still in operation
- B) Notify Command via emergency traffic
- C) Shut down the truck
- D) Replace the damaged hose section
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — fourth: the PCG increases the throttle to regain proper pressure for the lines still operating.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Fourth: increase the throttle to the proper pressure for the lines still in operation.

## Step E5 — Now notify (critical)
**Situation:** The first four are done — water is preserved for the interior crews. Now what?
- A) Shut down the truck
- B) Replace the damaged hose section
- C) Notify Command via emergency traffic so the interior companies know to exit the structure
- D) Keep pumping and wait for Command to call you
**Correct: C**
**If right:** Correct — now, and only now, notify Command via emergency traffic so the interior companies exit.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — The first four preserve water; the fifth step is the emergency-traffic notification. Shutting down or replacing hose before the interior crews are told to exit leaves them inside on a failing supply. Scenario over.

## Step E6 — Hold for PAR
**Situation:** Command has been notified and the interior companies are exiting. The truck is pumping on tank water. You:
- A) Shut down the truck now — the notification is done
- B) Hold — do not shut down until all interior companies are out and you have a PAR
- C) Replace the damaged hose section now
- D) Reopen the exterior lines to help the exiting crews
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — hold. Shutdown comes only when all interior companies are out and have a PAR.
**If wrong:** Wrong. You do not shut down — or replace hose — until every interior company is out with a PAR.

## Step E7 — Shutdown
**Situation:** All interior companies are out and you have a PAR. Now?
- A) Replace the damaged hose section first, then shut down
- B) Shut down the truck
- C) Reopen the intake valve
- D) Keep pumping on tank water until the hydrant is fixed
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — shut down the truck now that all interior companies are out with a PAR.
**If wrong:** Wrong. The list order is shutdown first, then hose replacement.

## Step E8 — Replace the hose
**Situation:** Truck is shut down. Replace the damaged section(s) in order:
- A) Place a new piece of hose in line, shut down the hydrant, restart the pumping sequence
- B) Shut down the hydrant, place a new piece of hose in line, restart the pumping sequence
- C) Restart the pumping sequence, then shut down the hydrant
- D) Shut down the hydrant, restart the pumping sequence, then place the new hose
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — shut the hydrant, new section in line, restart the pumping sequence.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Hydrant down first, new section in line, then restart the pumping sequence.

**Scenario E complete.** The pattern that saves crews: preserve water first (four steps), notify second, shut down on PAR, replace last.

# Scenario F: Pump Trouble on the Fireground

**Briefing:** The pump is misbehaving mid-operation: a dead PSG, a lost prime, an overheating pump, then an overheating engine. Work the troubleshooting pages in order. One rule governs the engine step: the truck does not shut down while crews are still inside.

## Step F1 — Dead PSG
**Situation:** The PSG will not work. What do you check?
- A) The tank-to-pump valve
- B) The cab/body throttle switch for the correct position (body throttle)
- C) The intake valve
- D) The discharge gauges
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — check the cab/body throttle switch for the correct position: body throttle.
**If wrong:** Wrong. A dead PSG means checking the cab/body throttle switch first — it must be on body throttle.

## Step F2 — Lost prime on tank water
**Situation:** You're on tank water and the pump loses prime. What do you check?
- A) Only whether there is water in the tank
- B) Is the truck in pump gear, is "tank-to-pump" open, is there water in the tank
- C) Only the intake valve
- D) The PSG throttle switch
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — all three: pump gear, "tank-to-pump" open, water in the tank.
**If wrong:** Wrong. On tank water you check three things: pump gear, "tank-to-pump" valve, water in the tank.

## Step F3 — Air in the pump
**Situation:** You found it — "tank-to-pump" was closed. It's open now. What may you still need to do?
- A) Open a discharge to allow air to escape
- B) Shut down the truck
- C) Open the tank fill fully
- D) Increase the throttle to maximum
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — after solving the problem, you may need to open a discharge to allow air to escape.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Once the cause is fixed, open a discharge so trapped air can escape and prime can re-establish.

## Step F4 — Lost prime on hydrant water
**Situation:** Now you're on hydrant water and prime is lost. What do you check?
- A) Is the "intake" valve open
- B) Is there water in the tank
- C) The cab/body throttle switch
- D) The tank fill valve
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — on hydrant water, check that the "intake" valve is open (then open a discharge for air if needed).
**If wrong:** Wrong. Hydrant water prime means one check first: is the "intake" valve open?

## Step F5 — Hot pipe
**Situation:** You place your hand on the intake piping where it enters the pump panel. It feels hot. What does that tell you?
- A) The pump is operating normally
- B) The pump is overheated — a centrifugal pump overheats if run too long without moving water through it
- C) The tank is empty
- D) The PSG has failed
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — warm or hot intake piping means the pump is overheated.
**If wrong:** Wrong. The hand-on-the-intake-pipe check: warm or hot means the pump is overheated.

## Step F6 — Cool the pump
**Situation:** The pump is overheated. Which three actions fix or avoid it?
- A) Take the truck out of pump gear when not pumping water, open the "tank fill" valve to recirculate water, open any discharge and flow water from the pump
- B) Increase the throttle, close all discharges, shut down the truck
- C) Open the tank fill fully and leave it open for the whole operation
- D) Shut down the hydrant and switch to tank water
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — all three: out of pump gear when idle, tank fill recirculation, or flow water from a discharge.
**If wrong:** Wrong. The three fixes: out of pump gear when not pumping, tank fill to recirculate, or open a discharge and flow water.

## Step F7 — Tank fill caution
**Situation:** You're flowing one handline and want to cool the pump with the tank fill. What is the danger of opening it fully?
- A) None — it only fills the tank
- B) It is equivalent to a 2 1/2" line flowing, robs water from your operation, and may cause the pump to cavitate even with only one handline operating
- C) It shuts down the pump automatically
- D) It drains the tank in seconds
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — tank fill fully open steals a 2 1/2" line worth of water and can cavitate the pump.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Tank fill equals a 2 1/2" line flowing — fully open, it robs your operation and may cavitate the pump even with one handline.

## Step F8 — Engine overheating (critical)
**Situation:** Now the ENGINE is overheating. Indirect coolers are open, a small hand line is spraying the radiator, and it is still overheating — but interior crews are still inside the structure. You:
- A) Shut down the truck immediately — the engine comes first
- B) Keep cooling and hold — shut down only after all interior crews are out and accounted for
- C) Open the tank fill fully to cool the engine
- D) Increase the throttle to push more water
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — hold. Shut down only after all interior crews are out and accounted for.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — Shutting down with crews inside kills their water and their way out. Cool it, hold, and shut down only once every interior crew is out and accounted for. Scenario over.

**Scenario F complete.** Troubleshoot in order, respect the tank-fill caution, and never trade the engine for crews still inside.

# Scenario G: Spotting & Driving

**Briefing:** You're driving and spotting the apparatus on a working fire: lay the line, spot the rig, hold your spacing, and drive the route by the book. Two calls end your day outright — parking under power lines and rolling a railroad crossing.

## Step G1 — Spot the tailboard
**Situation:** You're laying a supply line from the hydrant. Where do you spot the tailboard?
- A) A full block past the hydrant
- B) At or past the hydrant, within 10'
- C) Right at the fire building
- D) Anywhere on the hydrant's block
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — tailboard at or past the hydrant, within 10'.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Spot the tailboard at or past the hydrant, within 10 feet.

## Step G2 — Laying speeds
**Situation:** Line is going down. What speeds do you hold?
- A) 25 mph max, 10 mph on the fireground
- B) Maximum 15 mph, slowing to 3–5 mph on the fireground
- C) Posted speed until the fireground, then 15 mph
- D) 10 mph max the whole way
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — 15 mph max laying, 3–5 mph once on the fireground.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Max 15 mph laying the line; 3–5 mph on the fireground.

## Step G3 — Spot distance
**Situation:** The structure is 40 ft tall and there are no exposures to protect. How far away do you spot?
- A) 30' — the standard distance
- B) At least 40' — the height of the structure, since it is greater than 30'
- C) 20' — closer is better for the attack
- D) 50' — always add 10'
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — 30' or the structure height, whichever is greater. Here, 40'.
**If wrong:** Wrong. The rule is 30' OR structure height, whichever is greater — a 40 ft building means at least 40'.

## Step G4 — Deck gun and exposures
**Situation:** There are exposures on the B side. Where does the deck gun go?
- A) Anywhere with a clear view of the fire
- B) In a position to protect the exposures as needed
- C) Pointed at the main fire body
- D) It stays stowed unless the Captain orders it
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — place the deck gun to protect exposures as needed.
**If wrong:** Wrong. The deck gun's spot is driven by the exposures: place it to protect them.

## Step G5 — Power lines (critical)
**Situation:** The only flat, open spot near the fire is directly under power lines. You:
- A) Take it — a good spot beats the rule
- B) Refuse it and find another spot — you do not park under power lines
- C) Take it but keep the aerial stowed
- D) Take it if the Captain approves
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — never park under power lines, whatever the spot offers.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — "Do not park under power lines" is absolute. No spot is worth it. Scenario over.

## Step G6 — Spacing math
**Situation:** You're following in a 30 ft engine at 43 mph. How many seconds of space do you hold?
- A) 3 seconds
- B) 4 seconds
- C) 5 seconds
- D) 2 seconds
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — 1 second per 10 ft (3) plus 1 for being over 40 mph = 4 seconds.
**If wrong:** Wrong. One second per 10 ft of length is 3, plus one more over 40 mph: 4 seconds.

## Step G7 — Wheels in a turn
**Situation:** You're stopped, waiting to make a left turn. Where are your wheels?
- A) Turned left, ready to go
- B) Straight — left turn keeps wheels straight until you move
- C) Turned right to block the lane
- D) It doesn't matter while stopped
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — left turn: wheels straight while stopped. (Right turn: wheels face right.)
**If wrong:** Wrong. Stopped for a left turn, wheels stay straight; a right turn faces wheels right.

## Step G8 — Intersection, Code 3
**Situation:** You're driving through an intersection Code 3. What do you do with the brake, and what is your max speed entering?
- A) Cover the brake pedal; posted speed limit is the max
- B) Brake hard before every intersection; 10 over posted
- C) No brake needed if the light is green; 20 mph max
- D) Accelerate through to clear it faster; no limit
**Correct: A**
**If right:** Correct — cover the brake pedal, and the posted limit is the max entering the intersection Code 3.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Cover the brake pedal; posted speed is the max when entering an intersection Code 3.

## Step G9 — Railroad crossing (critical)
**Situation:** A railroad crossing is ahead on your route. What do you do?
- A) Roll through if no train is visible
- B) Stop — the same rule that applies to buses and Hazmat
- C) Slow to 20 mph and check LRL
- D) Stop only if the gates are down
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — traffic check (LRL), and you must stop, as buses and Hazmat must.
**If wrong:** ☠ FAIL — At a railroad crossing you stop. Rolling it in a fire apparatus is a critical error. Scenario over.

## Step G10 — Bridge check
**Situation:** Your route crosses an underpass. Before you commit, what do you check?
- A) Nothing — apparatus always fit
- B) Clearance / weight, and the height of your vehicle
- C) Only the posted speed
- D) Only whether traffic is clear
**Correct: B**
**If right:** Correct — clearance and weight limits against your vehicle's height before you go under.
**If wrong:** Wrong. Bridges and underpasses: check clearance / weight and know your vehicle's height first.

**Scenario G complete.** Spot for the exposures, hold your distance, hold your spacing, and never trade a rule for convenience.
