## 1. Air Brake Test (Pumper Pre-Trip Inspection)

**Q:** What is Step 1 of the air brake test?
**A:** Set wheel chocks properly.

**Q:** What is Step 2 of the air brake test?
**A:** Have engine off and power and ignition on.

**Q:** Step 3 — with the parking brake pushed in (released), what is the maximum allowable air drop after 1 minute?
**A:** No more than a 2 PSI drop.

**Q:** Step 4 — with the foot (service) brake applied, what is the maximum allowable initial drop in 1 minute?
**A:** No more than 3 PSI.

**Q:** 2 PSI vs 3 PSI — which leakage test is which?
**A:** 2 PSI = parking brake released (pushed in), 1 minute. 3 PSI = service/foot brake applied, 1 minute.

**Q:** Step 5 — when pumping the brake to test the low pressure warning signal, at what PSI should it activate?
**A:** 60 PSI (warning activates as pressure falls to 60 PSI).

**Q:** Step 6 — spring brakes activate between what pressures?
**A:** 20–40 PSI.

**Q:** Step 7 — at what engine speed do you check the air pressure rate of build-up?
**A:** High idle or 1500 RPM.

**Q:** Step 7 — how quickly must pressure build from 85 to 100 PSI?
**A:** Within 45 seconds.

**Q:** Step 7 — after reaching 85–100 PSI, what do you let it build to?
**A:** 120–125 PSI for cut-out.

**Q:** Step 8 — to check compressor cut-in, pump the brake down to what PSI, then what?
**A:** 100 PSI — then watch for the air build-up.

**Q:** Step 9 — compressor cut-out occurs between what pressures?
**A:** 120–125 PSI.

**Q:** Cut-in vs cut-out — which PSI is which?
**A:** Cut-in = 100 PSI (pump down to 100, watch it build). Cut-out = 120–125 PSI.

**Q:** What are Steps 10, 11, and 12, in order?
**A:** 10. Remove wheel chocks. 11. Test parking brake. 12. Test service brake.

**Q:** Why are wheel chocks set at Step 1 and removed at Step 10?
**A:** Chocks go on before the brake tests begin and come off only after the air system checks are complete, right before the final parking-brake and service-brake tests.

## 2. Code 3 Emergency Response Standards

**Q:** Code 3 Rule 1 — maximum speed over the posted limit?
**A:** 10 mph over the posted speed limit.

**Q:** Code 3 Rule 2 — when traveling in center or oncoming traffic lanes, what is the maximum speed, and what comes first?
**A:** A brief deceleration period first, then a maximum of 20 mph.

**Q:** Code 3 Rule 3 — in center or oncoming lanes, what do you do at stop signs and traffic lights?
**A:** Complete stop at ALL stop signs and traffic lights, regardless of the color of the light.

**Q:** Tricky: green light, but you are in the oncoming lane Code 3 — go or stop?
**A:** Stop. Complete stop regardless of the color of the light.

**Q:** Code 3 Rule 4 — when traveling WITH traffic, at what do you make a complete stop?
**A:** All red lights and stop signs.

**Q:** Code 3 Rule 5 — active school zones?
**A:** Code 3 lights and sirens may be used, but do NOT exceed the posted speed limit. Consider Code 2 when conditions warrant.

**Q:** With traffic at a green light — stop or go?
**A:** Go (Rule 4 requires stops only at red lights and stop signs when traveling with traffic).

## 3. Phase 2 Pumping Exercise

**Q:** In Phase 2, who takes the key pumper position?
**A:** The candidate.

**Q:** Who represents the forward pumper?
**A:** The first engineer of drivers training.

**Q:** Who handjacks supply hose to the key pumper?
**A:** The second engineer.

**Q:** Who is responsible for making all necessary connections?
**A:** The candidate.

**Q:** How does the exercise begin, and when does time start?
**A:** Get in the truck and properly put it in pump. Exit with a safety vest and radio. Time starts as soon as the foot hits the ground.

**Q:** What is the first priority once time starts?
**A:** Properly pump the humat.

**Q:** What is the exact hose-and-coupling sequence to the humat?
**A:** 1. Deploy the first length of hose, connect it to the front of the humat. 2. Grab the double male from the compartment, attach it to the hose. 3. Deploy the second length of hose, connect it to the first length using the double male. 4. Grab the second double male, connect it to your intake, then to your second hose.

**Q:** After the humat connections, what goes where at the rear of the truck?
**A:** Walk to the rear, pull a length of supply line: female end to the truck discharge (passenger N.1), male end to the side of the humat.

**Q:** What is the exact valve-opening sequence once connections are made?
**A:** 1. Open the discharge connected to the humat FIRST. 2. Flip the butterfly valve. 3. Then open the intake (keystone).

**Q:** At what pressure do you pump until the forward pumper calls for more water?
**A:** Equal pressure to your intake pressure.

**Q:** What is the maximum pump pressure in Phase 2?
**A:** 150 psi.

**Q:** For the 2nd water supply, what couplings are needed for every reverse operation?
**A:** A double male AND a double female.

**Q:** From the key pumper, which coupling connects to the discharge in the reverse operation?
**A:** The double female.

**Q:** Before sending water, what must the engineer do?
**A:** Communicate face-to-face or over the radio that they are ready for water.

**Q:** When is it appropriate to place wheel chocks in Phase 2?
**A:** After opening the discharge, while standing by to receive orders from the practor.

**Q:** What is the full Phase 2 shutdown sequence?
**A:** 1. All crews accounted for and out of the building before shutting down lines. 2. Communicate with the other engineers that shutdown is beginning. 3. Bring the pump to 100 psi or less before idle. 4. Close all gates. 5. Open tank fill to fill tank and cool pump. 6. Feel the water on the ground — must be cool before closing tank fill. 7. Shut down key system. 8. Put away wheel chocks. 9. Take vehicle out of pump properly. 10. Shut down truck. Time stops.

**Q:** List the 5 Critical Factors of Phase 2.
**A:** 1. Never allow intake level to drop below 20–30 psi. 2. All crews out, accounted for, and safe before shutting down lines. 3. Receiving trucks ready before sending water. 4. Always send big water slow and controlled. 5. Truck in NEUTRAL when operating a 2-stage pump — neutral going in / neutral going out.

**Q:** What gear must the truck be in when operating the 2-stage pump?
**A:** NEUTRAL — neutral going in, neutral going out.

## 4. Engineer Practical Test Checklist

### Cab Preparation

**Q:** What are the 5 cab-prep steps, in order?
**A:** 1. In the truck with seatbelt fastened and safety vest on. 2. Start truck, verify Neutral. 3. Engage Dual Stage PTO. 4. Shift to Drive, verify two green lights. 5. Remove seatbelt, grab radio, look both ways.

**Q:** After shifting to Drive, what must you verify?
**A:** Two green lights.

### Initial Setup

**Q:** Where do you stop, and what do you verbalize?
**A:** Stop at the front tire and verbalize that wheel chocks will be set later.

**Q:** What must be verified OPEN before opening a discharge at idle?
**A:** Tank-to-Pump.

**Q:** What pump mode do you set, and at what PSI do you pump the handline?
**A:** PSI Mode, at the PSI specified in your briefing (the checklist leaves this blank — know your assigned number).

### Water Supply

**Q:** Where do the two supply sections get deployed?
**A:** One supply section to the top of the truck, one to the Keystone.

**Q:** How do you open the hose clamp, and what must you NOT do?
**A:** Open it facing the hazard. DO NOT kick it open.

**Q:** After opening the Keystone, what do you verify?
**A:** That the RPM drops.

**Q:** After the Keystone, what three things do you do?
**A:** Crack Tank Fill and mark gauges, set wheel chocks, listen for radio traffic.

### Ladder Operations

**Q:** Before opening ANY discharge, what must you verify — and what happens if you get it wrong?
**A:** Verify you have identified the CORRECT discharge. Opening the wrong discharge = AUTOMATIC FAIL.

**Q:** Before opening the ladder discharge, what two things must you confirm?
**A:** Ladder drains are closed and the ladder is ready for water.

**Q:** While pumping the ladder, what do you do with the handline, and what do you reference?
**A:** Gate down the handline; reference your gauge mark.

### Safety Checks

**Q:** What is the minimum intake pressure to maintain?
**A:** Above 20 PSI.

**Q:** What three safety checks are listed?
**A:** Maintain intake above 20 PSI, mark supply lines for safety, check for kinks.

**Q:** If supply lines begin to separate, what do you deploy?
**A:** An additional 100 ft of supply hose, with the hose clamp placed upstream of the failure.

### Shutdown

**Q:** What must be confirmed before beginning shutdown?
**A:** PAR is complete and all crews are out of the hot zone.

**Q:** What pressure do you reduce to before idling?
**A:** Below 100 PSI.

**Q:** Tank Fill is opened for what two reasons?
**A:** 1. Fill the tank. 2. Cool the pump.

**Q:** How do you verify the pump is cool enough before closing Tank Fill?
**A:** Feel the water — if it is still hot, continue circulating water for a few more minutes.

**Q:** What is the exact order of the shutdown sequence?
**A:** 1. Confirm PAR + crews out of hot zone. 2. Reduce below 100 PSI, idle. 3. Close all discharge lines. 4. Open Tank Fill (fill + cool). 5. Feel water; keep cooling if hot. 6. 360° walk-around. 7. Close Keystone. 8. Verify gauges/panel, all valves closed. 9. Store wheel chocks. 10. Neutral → wait 5 sec → PTO first position → wait 5 sec → PTO final position. 11. Double-check gauges, shut down truck.

**Q:** Recite the exact Neutral/PTO disengagement sequence.
**A:** Neutral — wait 5 seconds — PTO first position — wait 5 seconds — PTO final position.

### Critical Verbalizations (say them out loud)

**Q:** What are the critical verbalizations?
**A:** "Wheel chocks will be set later." / Identify the crosslay being pumped. / "Opening the wrong discharge is an automatic FAIL." / "Do NOT kick the hose clamp open." / "Ladder drains are closed and the ladder is ready for water before opening the discharge." / "Maintain intake pressure above 20 PSI." / "Mark supply lines for safety." / "PAR complete and all crews out of the hot zone before shutdown." / "Tank Fill: fill the tank and cool the pump — feel the water."
